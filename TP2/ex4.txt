As the compression factor increases, the recovered image 
becomes more simillar to the original image.

With a complression factor of 50, the 8x8 blocks start to merge 
into 16x8 blocks. 
With a compression factor of 100, the recovered 
image's objects starts to become more recognizable, although still 
clearly divided into chuncks.
With a compression factor of 200, the chuncks start to merge 
into a consistent image.
With a compression factor of 1000, the recovered image is almost 
the same as the original image with just a couple color artifacts.

On the other hand, reducing the compression factor reduces the 
recovered image's recognizability even further, even destroying 
the image completly with a complression factor of 1, and even 
creating only a few colored blocks, with a compression factor of 
10.
